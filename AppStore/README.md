# App Store metadata

The App Store Connect texts, kept in the project. Nothing is uploaded
automatically — open the file, copy the contents, paste it into the matching
field in App Store Connect.

The folders are named after the fastlane convention (`cs`, `en-US`, `en-GB`), so
if you ever decide to move to `fastlane deliver`, renaming `AppStore/` to
`fastlane/metadata/` is all it takes.

## Where everything goes

App Store Connect → **Apps → Solitaire → iOS App → 1.0 Prepare for Submission**.
There is a language switch at the top — every field is filled in separately for
each language.

| Field in App Store Connect | File | Limit | Currently |
|---|---|---|---|
| App Name | `<language>/name.txt` | 30 characters | 26 / 27 / 27 |
| Subtitle | `<language>/subtitle.txt` | 30 characters | 27 / 29 / 26 |
| Promotional Text | `<language>/promotional_text.txt` | 170 characters | 164 / 165 / 165 |
| Description | `<language>/description.txt` | 4000 characters | ~2900 |
| Keywords | `<language>/keywords.txt` | 100 characters | 97 / 97 / 98 |
| What's New in This Version | `<language>/release_notes.txt` | 4000 characters | ~570 |
| App Preview and Screenshots | generated by the scripts, see [screenshots.md](screenshots.md) | 1–10 images | 8 + video |

(The three numbers are `cs` / `en-US` / `en-GB`, in that order.)

Alternative names, subtitles and keyword sets, the reasoning behind the ones in
use, and what the competition looks like are in
[alternatives.md](alternatives.md).

Checking it. The limits are the easy half — the three checks after them are the
ones that catch real mistakes: a word paid for twice across two indexed fields, a
stray space in the keyword list, and a paragraph left wrapped at 80 columns.

```bash
python3 - <<'PY'
import os,re
lim={'name.txt':30,'subtitle.txt':30,'keywords.txt':100,
     'promotional_text.txt':170,'description.txt':4000,'release_notes.txt':4000}
bad=0
for loc in ('cs','en-US','en-GB'):
    d=lambda f:open(os.path.join('AppStore',loc,f),encoding='utf-8').read().rstrip('\n')
    for f,m in lim.items():
        n=len(d(f)); bad+=n>m
        print(f'{loc:6}{f:22}{n:5}/{m}', 'OK' if n<=m else 'OVER!')
    # a word in two indexed fields is paid for twice and indexed once
    t=lambda f:set(re.findall(r'[\wÀ-ſ]+', d(f).lower()))
    for a,b in (('name.txt','subtitle.txt'),('name.txt','keywords.txt'),
                ('subtitle.txt','keywords.txt')):
        ov=t(a)&t(b); bad+=bool(ov)
        if ov: print(f'{loc:6}DUPLICATED {a} x {b}: {sorted(ov)}')
    # a space after a comma silently costs one of the 100 characters
    if ', ' in d('keywords.txt'): bad+=1; print(f'{loc:6}KEYWORDS: space after comma')
    # App Store Connect keeps newlines - a paragraph has to be a single line
    L=d('description.txt').split('\n')
    for i,l in enumerate(L[:-1]):
        if (l.strip() and L[i+1].strip() and not l.isupper() and not l.startswith(chr(8226))
                and not L[i+1].startswith(chr(8226))
                and not l.rstrip().endswith(('.','!','?',':','…'))):
            bad+=1; print(f'{loc:6}DESCRIPTION line {i+1} wrapped mid-paragraph')
print('ALL CLEAN' if not bad else f'{bad} problem(s)')
PY
```

Run it from the repository root, not from this directory.

## Which localisations to create, and why three

Keywords never combine across languages — each localisation is its own search
index — but **most storefronts index two of them**, so a second language is free
extra keyword space in the same country rather than a different market.

| Storefront | Indexes | What that buys |
|---|---|---|
| United States | English (U.S.) + Spanish (Mexico) | `es-MX` would double the US pool |
| Czechia | **Czech + English (U.K.)** | `en-GB` doubles the Czech pool |
| United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand | English (U.K.) | `en-GB` is the primary there |

So the three folders here are:

- **`en-US`** — primary language of the record, and what most English storefronts
  read.
- **`cs`** — primary in Czechia, and the one worth the most effort per hour: the
  Czech solitaire market is nearly unlocalised (see
  [alternatives.md](alternatives.md)).
- **`en-GB`** — primary in the UK/Ireland/Australia/New Zealand *and* the second
  index in Czechia. Same description as `en-US` with British spelling, and a
  subtitle built around *patience* rather than *classic*, because outside North
  America that is what people call the game.

`es-MX` is the obvious next one (it would double the US keyword pool for the cost
of a translation), and it is *not* included here: the app's interface is English
and Czech only, so a Spanish-speaking visitor would arrive at an English app.
That is allowed, and plenty of apps do it, but it trades conversion for reach.
Worth trying once there is enough traffic to measure the trade rather than guess
at it.

## Notes on the individual fields

- **App Name** — has to be globally unique, and it is the single strongest
  ranking signal. Plain "Solitaire" belongs to MobilityWare and has for fifteen
  years, hence `Solitaire Klondike: Offline`: it carries *solitaire*, *klondike*
  and *offline* while still reading like a title. On the home screen the app stays
  just "Solitaire" / "Solitér" (`INFOPLIST_KEY_CFBundleDisplayName` and
  `InfoPlist.xcstrings`), which Apple does not mind — the home screen name only
  must not differ in *meaning*. That constraint is why the Czech store name leads
  with *Solitér* rather than *Solitaire*.
- **Subtitle** — the second strongest signal and the line people actually read in
  search results. It adds the terms the name could not fit (*patience*,
  *classic*, *no ads*) rather than repeating it.
- **Keywords** — separate with commas and **no spaces** (a space counts towards
  the limit). Do not repeat words already in the name or subtitle, Apple indexes
  those separately; that is why *solitaire*, *klondike*, *offline*, *classic* and
  *patience* are missing from the English lists.
- **Promotional Text** — the only field that can be changed without a new build
  and without review. Useful for seasonal or topical messages.
- **What's New** — for a first release the field often does not appear at all; if
  App Store Connect does not offer it, skip it and use it from version 1.1 on.

## Settings that are not filled in with text

**App Information** (shared across all languages):

- Primary Category: **Games → Card**
- Secondary Category: **Games → Board** — which is where MobilityWare, nerByte and
  Tripledot all put their solitaires. Games → Casino is where the poker app sits
  and would be wrong here: this game has no wagering of any kind, and sitting next
  to the slot machines would undercut the 4+ rating the listing depends on.
- Content Rights: contains no third-party content
- **Gambling: No.** No real money, no wagering, nothing to buy.

`LSApplicationCategoryType` is **not** set in the project. It has no effect on the
App Store listing — it is the Mac App Store key — so nothing breaks, but setting
`public.app-category.card-games` costs one line and keeps the project honest
about what it is:

```
INFOPLIST_KEY_LSApplicationCategoryType = public.app-category.card-games;
```

**Age Rating: 4+, and it is worth understanding why.** In the Chance-Based
Activities step, Simulated Gambling is answered **None**. Klondike is a card game
of chance, but there is nothing to wager and nothing to win: no bet is placed, no
odds are offered, and no balance can be topped up or cashed out. Vegas mode is a
*scoring convention* — the deck notionally costs 52 and each foundation card pays
5 — not a gambling mechanic, and the numbers behave exactly like a score that can
go negative. Microsoft's own Solitaire Collection ships Vegas scoring for Klondike
and is rated 4+; so are Solitaire.com and half the top ten. The apps in the
category that carry 12+ or 17+ are almost all carrying it for their *advertising*,
which this app does not have. Everything else in the questionnaire is None: no
violence, no chat, no links out, no contests, no loot boxes, no unrestricted web
access, no user-generated content.

If a reviewer ever pushes back on Vegas mode, the honest answer is *Infrequent*
rather than *Frequent* — but do not volunteer it. Answering it at all would give
12+ and take the app out of every family account for the sake of a scoreboard
that counts in dollars. The point is made in
[review-notes.txt](review-notes.txt) so the question gets answered before it is
asked.

**Pricing and Availability:** Free, all countries. There is no Korean GRAC
problem here — that one applies to simulated gambling, which this app does not
have.

**App Privacy:** click *Get Started* → **"No, we do not collect data from this
app"**. The game has no networking code, no third-party SDKs, and stores
everything in UserDefaults on the device (declared in
`solitaire/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`, reason CA92.1). A **privacy policy URL is
still mandatory** — the text is ready in [privacy-policy/](privacy-policy/), it
just needs hosting somewhere (GitHub Pages, a gist, your own site) and the URL
pasted into the *Privacy Policy URL* field.

**Support URL:** a mandatory field. The project's GitHub page or a simple page
with an e-mail address will do.

**Marketing URL:** optional, feel free to leave it empty.

**Encryption:** `INFOPLIST_KEY_ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO` is already set
in the project, so the export compliance question never appears when the build is
uploaded.

**App Review Information** → the note for the reviewer is in
[review-notes.txt](review-notes.txt). The game needs neither an account nor a
demo login — leave *Sign-in required* switched off.

## Release procedure

1. Xcode → Signing & Capabilities, check the team in `Local.xcconfig` and the
   bundle ID `cz.rob.solitaire`.
2. App Store Connect → **Apps → +** → new record, bundle ID `cz.rob.solitaire`,
   primary language **English (U.S.)** (the fallback for every storefront without
   its own localisation, so this is the one that decides reach), SKU e.g.
   `solitaire-001`.
3. Add the other two localisations (App Information → language switch, top
   right): **Czech** and **English (U.K.)**.
4. Fill in the texts from this directory. The screenshots and App Preview are
   not in the repo — build them with `Tools/appstore_media.sh` and upload them,
   see [screenshots.md](screenshots.md) for details. The iPhone slot this
   listing uses is **6.5"**, not 6.9". `en-GB` has no media of its own; upload
   the `en-US` set into it.
5. Xcode → Product → Archive → Distribute App → App Store Connect → Upload.
6. Wait for the build to process (10–60 min), then select it in the *Build*
   section.
7. Fill in App Privacy, Age Rating, Pricing.
8. **Add for Review** → *Submit*.
